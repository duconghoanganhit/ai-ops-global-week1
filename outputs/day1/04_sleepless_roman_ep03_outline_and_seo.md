## 2. 6-Chapter Historical Beat Sheet (Ready to Expand to 1,900-Word Script)

### Intro [00:00 – 01:15] — Stepping Out of the Autumn Rain (216 AD) (~160 words)
- Set the year: 216 AD, shortly after Emperor Caracalla's monumental public baths (*Thermae Antoninianae*) opened on Rome's southern edge near the Via Appia.
- Contrast: outside is grey October rain and muddy cobblestones; inside are twenty-five acres of gardens, vaulted brick domes, warm marble, and hushed echoing water.
- Sensory anchor: rain tapping high stone windows, smell of warm mineral water, pine resin, and sweet olive oil.

### Chapter 1 [01:15 – 03:30] — One Quadrans at the Gate & the Apodyterium (~310 words)
- Admission fee: a single *quadrans* (a quarter of an *as*, the smallest Roman copper coin — sometimes completely free on festival days).
- Leaving heavy damp *paenula* wool cloaks and leather sandals in recessed stone/wood wall niches inside the *apodyterium* (changing room).
- Historical human detail: paying a trustworthy *capsarius* (cloak-attendant) a tiny tip to watch your tunic and coin pouch, since bathhouse thieves were a recurring annoyance noted by Roman poets like Martial and Catullus.
- Slipping on simple wooden-soled bath clogs (*soleae*) so bare feet wouldn't scorch on the hottest floors ahead.

### Chapter 2 [03:30 – 05:45] — Beneath the Floor: The Quiet Fire of the Hypocaust (~310 words)
- Pull the camera softly underground into the vaulted service tunnels below the baths — wide enough for supply carts delivering tons of dry hardwood logs daily.
- Explain the *praefurnia* (dozens of steady wood furnaces) feeding hot air through hollow brick pillars (*pilae*) supporting concrete and marble mosaic floors, plus vertical terracotta box flues (*tubuli*) hidden inside the walls.
- Keep the tone peaceful and awe-inspiring: no roaring industrial machinery — just the slow, steady radiant warmth of heated stone and vast lead-lined water boilers fed by a dedicated branch of the Aqua Marcia aqueduct.

### Chapter 3 [05:45 – 08:05] — The Tepidarium: Where Steam Softens the Afternoon (~315 words)
- Entering the *tepidarium* (warm room) first so the body acclimates gently without thermal shock.
- Describe the light: diffuse grey afternoon daylight filtering through high arched windows lined with translucent thin sheets of mica or early cast glass, catching ribbons of pale steam.
- Quiet acoustics: soft murmur of neighbors resting on warm marble benches, a gentle trickle of water from bronze lion-head spouts, zero rush.

### Chapter 4 [08:05 – 10:25] — Olive Oil, the Bronze Strigil, and the Hot Caldarium (~315 words)
- Moving into the grand circular *caldarium* (hot room) crowned by a soaring dome nearly the width of the Pantheon.
- Historical authenticity: Romans didn't lather up with modern scented soap bars. Instead, bathers poured a small measure of warm olive oil from a tiny glass or bronze flask (*aryballos*) over their shoulders and arms, letting steam loosen dust and fatigue.
- Using the curved bronze *strigil* to gently scrape away oil, sweat, and road dust, rinsing cleanly beside the shallow marble *labrum* (cold-water splash basin) before sinking quietly into a deep steaming immersion pool.

### Chapter 5 [10:25 – 12:15] — The Cool Marble Echo of the Frigidarium (~260 words)
- Stepping slowly back through the warm room into the soaring cross-vaulted *frigidarium* (cold hall) — granite columns, green and porphyry marble, still mirror-dark plunge pools.
- The calming sensation of cool water closing the pores after deep heat, followed by wrapping up in a thick dry linen towel (*linteum*).
- Mention the quieter side rooms off the courtyard: Greek and Latin libraries where older citizens sat quietly listening to rain on colonnade roofs.

### Chapter 6 [12:15 – 13:30] — Walking Home Through Dusk with Warm Shoulders (~210 words)
- Stepping back into the *apodyterium*, pulling on dry layered wool tunics and hooded cloak, reclaiming a warm glow that stays in the bones for hours.
- Walking home as rain slows to a fine mist over the Via Appia; oil lamps lighting up shopfronts; settling into quiet rest with muscles heavy and warm. Soft "Good night."
